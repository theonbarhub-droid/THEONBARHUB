Passing the LSO Barrister and Solicitor licensing exams is the milestone most candidates focus on. But the moment you actually become a lawyer in Ontario is the Call to the Bar ceremony. This guide covers what it is, what to expect, and how to prepare.

## What Call to the Bar Is

Call to the Bar is the formal admission ceremony run by the Law Society of Ontario. When you are "called," you take an oath of office, receive your lawyer's license, and become a member of the LSO with the right to practise law in Ontario.

It is a ceremony, not a test. By the time you are called, all requirements are complete: you have passed both licensing exams, finished your articling placement or its equivalent, and the LSO has verified everything. The ceremony marks the transition from licensing candidate to lawyer.

## Who Gets Called

To be called to the bar you need to have:

- Completed the Law Practice Program or an articling placement
- Passed both the Barrister and Solicitor licensing examinations
- Satisfied all character and fitness requirements
- Paid all outstanding LSO fees

Candidates who complete their licensing process in one sitting are called together at a ceremony scheduled after results are released. The LSO groups candidates by completion date, so not everyone passes on the same cycle.

## When It Happens

The LSO holds multiple Call to the Bar ceremonies each year, coordinated with exam cycles. After your results come in and all administrative requirements are confirmed, the LSO will contact you with your ceremony date and instructions.

For candidates completing the process after the fall 2026 exams, results arrive in December and January. Ceremonies for that cohort typically take place in the spring.

## What Happens at the Ceremony

The ceremony follows a formal structure:

**Before the ceremony:** You arrive early, collect your gown, and are organized with other candidates being called the same day. There is usually a brief orientation.

**The oath:** Candidates stand before a judge or LSO bencher and take the oath of office. This is the moment you formally become a lawyer. You commit to upholding the law and meeting your obligations to clients, the court, and the profession.

**The certificate:** You receive your certificate of admission as a barrister and solicitor of the courts of Ontario.

**The gown:** Wearing the barrister's gown during the ceremony is traditional. The LSO arranges gown rental as part of the ceremony process.

**Photographs:** Most ceremonies allow time for photographs after the formal proceedings. Many candidates arrange professional photography in the gown with family.

## Guests and Tickets

Guests are limited due to venue capacity. The LSO allocates tickets to each candidate and you choose who to invite within your allocation. Typically candidates bring immediate family.

The LSO sends detailed information about guest tickets when it confirms your ceremony date. Do not buy tickets from anyone else or assume you have more than your allocation.

## What to Wear

Under the gown, business formal attire is standard. For many candidates this means a suit. The LSO's ceremony package will include specific guidance.

If you need to rent or purchase a barrister's gown for other purposes later, suppliers in Toronto cater to newly called lawyers.

## After the Ceremony

Being called is the beginning, not the end. After the ceremony:

**Licensing:** Your name appears in the LSO's public directory of lawyers. You are now a licensed lawyer in Ontario.

**Annual fee:** Your LSO annual fee becomes due. Make sure it is paid before you practise.

**Insurance:** Errors and omissions insurance is mandatory. If you are joining a firm, the firm carries group coverage. If you are practising independently, you need your own policy through the LawPRO program.

**Articling obligations:** If you have outstanding obligations from your articling placement, those continue until completed.

**Business cards and trust accounts:** If you are going into practice, you can now put "Barrister and Solicitor" on your letterhead and business cards.

## Practical Notes

**Photographs:** Plan for photographs before or after the ceremony. The gown is only worn for the ceremony itself, so if you want photos in it, arrange them that day.

**Travel:** If family is coming from out of town, book accommodation early. Toronto venues for large ceremonies fill quickly.

**Social media:** Many newly called lawyers post about their call. The LSO does not restrict this but be mindful of professional obligations that begin the moment you are called.

**The feeling:** Many lawyers describe Call to the Bar as genuinely moving. After years of school, the NCA process or law school in Canada, articling, and exams, the ceremony marks a real transition. It is worth being present for it.
