Accommodation requests for the LSO licensing exams are more time-sensitive than most candidates realize. The key trap: the accommodation deadline for the Winter 2027 sitting comes **before** fall 2026 results are released. This guide explains how accommodations work and what you need to do.

## What Accommodations Are

Accommodations are adjustments to standard exam conditions for candidates with documented disabilities or medical conditions. They are not an advantage. They are a correction that gives you the same opportunity to demonstrate your knowledge as candidates who do not face the same barriers.

The LSO provides accommodations under its duty to accommodate. Common adjustments include:

- Extra time (commonly 1.5x or 2x the standard time)
- A private or small-group testing room
- Scheduled rest breaks
- Assistive technology (screen readers, speech-to-text software)
- Large-print materials
- A scribe or reader

The specific accommodations available depend on your documented need. Each request is assessed individually.

## The Deadline Trap

This is the most important thing to understand about accommodations for the LSO bar exams.

The accommodation deadline is **not** the same as the registration deadline, and it is not tied to your exam result. It is a separate, earlier date.

For the **Winter 2027 sitting**:
- Barrister exam: February 25, 2027
- Solicitor exam: March 11, 2027
- Accommodation request deadline: **January 12, 2027**

Fall 2026 Solicitor results are estimated to arrive between January 6 and 13, 2027. The accommodation deadline for the winter sitting is January 12.

This means if you wrote the November 2026 Solicitor exam and need accommodations for the March 2027 retake, you may need to submit your accommodation request **before you know whether you passed the November sitting**.

Candidates who wait for their result and then try to request accommodations for the next sitting frequently miss the deadline.

## What Qualifies

Accommodations are for documented disabilities and medical conditions. This includes but is not limited to:

- Learning disabilities (dyslexia, dyscalculia)
- Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)
- Anxiety disorders
- Physical disabilities affecting writing or stamina
- Visual or hearing impairments
- Chronic illness affecting concentration or endurance

The condition must be documented by a qualified professional and must affect your ability to perform under standard exam conditions. A current diagnosis from your treating professional is typically required.

The LSO does not grant accommodations based on stress, general test anxiety without a documented condition, or difficulty with the subject matter.

## Documentation Requirements

The LSO requires documentation that:

1. Confirms your diagnosis from a qualified professional (psychologist, physician, or relevant specialist)
2. Describes how the condition affects your performance in timed, high-stakes examination settings
3. Recommends specific accommodations with a rationale

Documentation requirements are specific. The LSO's accommodation request form and instructions outline exactly what is needed. Read those requirements carefully before gathering your documents.

Older assessments may not be accepted. The LSO often requires assessments conducted within a specified number of years, and adult assessments are generally required rather than childhood assessments.

## How to Apply

1. Obtain the accommodation request form from the LSO website.
2. Have your treating professional complete the clinical portion of the form or provide a letter that meets the LSO's requirements.
3. Submit everything by the accommodation deadline for the sitting you are registering for.
4. The LSO reviews requests and communicates its decision before the exam.

Apply early. If your documentation is incomplete, you will need time to gather additional information before the deadline.

## If You Already Have Accommodations from Law School

Having received accommodations at a law school or university does not automatically carry over to the LSO exams. You need to apply through the LSO's process. That said, existing documentation from your school's accessibility office may be useful. Check whether the LSO will accept it or whether it needs to be updated.

## If You Miss the Deadline

Late requests are generally not accepted. If you have a documented need, apply by the deadline even under uncertainty about whether you will need to write the exam.

If you miss the deadline due to circumstances outside your control, contact the LSO directly. There is no guarantee of an exception, but exceptional circumstances are sometimes considered.

## Confidentiality

Accommodation requests and approvals are confidential. They do not appear on your license, in the public LSO directory, or on any certificate or record provided to employers. Using accommodations has no effect on how your result is recorded.

## Practical Advice

If you have a documented disability or medical condition:

- Apply for accommodations for every sitting you register for, even if you hope to pass without needing them.
- Do not wait for your result before thinking about the next sitting.
- Keep your documentation current. If your assessment is several years old, consider whether an updated assessment is needed.
- Contact the LSO's accommodation office early if you have questions about what qualifies or what documentation is required.

The accommodation process exists for a reason. If you have a legitimate need, use it.
