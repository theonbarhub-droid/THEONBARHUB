A result that is not a pass hurts, especially after months of work. It is also common, and it is fixable in the time you have. If your November 17, 2026 Solicitor result is not what you hoped, this page gives you the dates that matter and a plan for the **March 11, 2027** sitting.

## The dates that matter

| What | Date |
| --- | --- |
| Estimated November Solicitor results | January 6 to 13, 2027 ([how we estimate](/blog/when-are-ontario-bar-exam-results-released)) |
| Accommodation requests for winter sittings | January 12, 2027 |
| Register, defer or cancel for March | **January 27, 2027** |
| Winter Solicitor exam | **Thursday, March 11, 2027** |
| New 2027-28 materials released | April 13, 2027 |
| Summer Solicitor exam (next option after March) | June 22, 2027 |

Two traps to avoid:

1. **The accommodation deadline can arrive before your result.** If you think you may need an accommodation for March, start that conversation before results day.
2. **Waiting until summer is not free.** The June 2027 sitting uses new 2027-28 materials, and summer sittings fall in the next licensing year, with its own application deadline of March 1, 2027. March lets you reuse everything you already have.

Always confirm your own eligibility, attempts and fees in LSO Connects. Dates are from the [LSO's exam dates page](https://lso.ca/becoming-licensed/lawyer-licensing-process/licensing-examinations/examination-dates-and-deadlines).

## Why repeat writers usually fall short

Across thousands of public posts by Ontario candidates, the same few causes come up again and again when people explain a failed attempt:

- **Time ran out.** Not finishing, or rushing the last block, comes up constantly.
- **Finding the answer took too long.** The exam is open book, but candidates who could not locate the right page quickly lost marks they knew.
- **One area dragged the score down.** Often a single subject, such as Real Estate or Business Law, carried most of the misses.
- **Practice scores were close, not comfortable.** Among the small group of candidates who shared both practice scores and later results, those who went on to pass averaged about 72.8% on practice tests; those who did not averaged about 68.5%. It is a small sample, but the gap is a useful target: aim to be clearly above 70% under timed conditions before exam day.

The plan below attacks all four.

## The 8-week plan

### Week 1: diagnose before you study

- Read your result feedback closely. Note which areas were weakest.
- Write one full timed practice exam, cold. Do not review notes first.
- Sort every miss into one of three causes: **did not know the rule**, **could not find it in time**, or **misread the question**. Most repeat writers discover that "could not find it" and "misread" cost more than "did not know".

### Weeks 2 to 5: rebuild the weak areas

- Spend about 70% of your time on the two weakest subjects and 30% keeping the others warm.
- For every miss caused by "could not find it", go back to the index and practise locating that topic until you can do it in under a minute.
- Do short timed sets every day (20 to 40 questions), not marathon reading sessions.
- Keep a running list of your mistakes in your own words. Review it every few days.

### Weeks 6 and 7: full exam rehearsal

- Write at least two full-length timed practice exams under real conditions: same start time, same breaks, your own tabbed materials, no phone.
- Track your pace by section. Decide in advance how long you allow yourself per question before you mark it and move on.
- After each mock, repeat the three-cause sort. Your "could not find it" misses should be shrinking.

### Week 8: taper and logistics

- Lighter review of your mistake list and index tabs. No new material.
- Confirm your exam location, arrival time and what you are allowed to bring.
- Sleep on a normal schedule for the whole week, not just the night before.

## If the result was close

If you were near the line, do not start over from page one of the materials. The marks you need are almost always in speed, navigation and one or two weak areas. Spending eight weeks rereading everything is the most common way repeat writers end up with a similar result.
